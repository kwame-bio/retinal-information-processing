Retinal Information Processing

A computational neuroscience portfolio exploring how retinal systems encode, filter, and organize biological information using Python, signal analysis, dimensionality reduction, and machine learning.

Research Focus

This project investigates the retina as an information-processing system. Through computational simulations, I explore how visual signals move through retinal layers, how noise affects biological fidelity, and how mathematical tools can reveal structure in neural activity.

This project began with basic signal visualization and expanded into a structured computational investigation of retinal information processing. The notebooks model neural variability, dimensionality reduction, frequency structure, receptive field organization, spike trains, filtering, and clustering.

Future work includes integrating experimentally recorded retinal datasets, analyzing neural population activity, exploring real retinal gene-expression data, and building more biologically realistic models of visual encoding.

Kwame Osei Senior is an undergraduate student interested in computational biology, neuroscience, and visual information processing. This portfolio documents his independent exploration of how biological systems preserve and transform information.
