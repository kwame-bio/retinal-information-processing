Retinal Information Processing

Computational Investigation of Neural Signal Encoding

How does the retina encode and preserve visual information despite biological noise? This research investigates retinal neural systems through signal processing, machine learning, and information theory to understand the computational principles of visual encoding.

About This Research

Research Overview

The vertebrate retina processes approximately 130 million photons per second, yet transmits visual information through only 1 million nerve fibers to the brain. This represents an extreme data compression problem: how does the retina achieve reliable visual encoding while compressing information by 130-fold and dealing with constant biological noise?

Approach

I developed a computational pipeline to simulate retinal neural dynamics and apply multiple analytical techniques to understand information processing. The analysis includes signal simulation, noise characterization, dimensionality reduction, frequency analysis, receptive field modeling, machine learning clustering, and information-theoretic analysis across 14 interconnected Jupyter notebooks.

Motivation

  • Retinal degeneration affects millions globally—understanding noise effects could inform treatment strategies
  • Reveals fundamental principles of neural coding applicable across sensory systems
  • Provides computational foundation for visual prosthetics and vision restoration research
  • Demonstrates sophisticated neuroscience research conducted independently at undergraduate level

Background

Conducted independently during 2025-2026, this research was built from scratch using Python and standard open-source libraries. All code and analyses are publicly available for verification and reproducibility. The goal was to investigate a genuine neuroscience question through rigorous computational methodology.

Current Research Questions

Signal Fidelity

How do retinal systems preserve useful information under noisy biological conditions?

Neural Encoding

How can spike trains and retinal activity patterns encode visual information over time?

Biological Organization

Can computational analysis reveal hidden functional structure in retinal neural activity?

Information Efficiency

How efficiently can retinal systems encode visual information relative to theoretical limits?

Key Findings

Low-Rank Structure

Retinal neural activity concentrates in 3-5 principal components, explaining 95%+ of variance. Suggests fundamental efficiency principle in neural computation.

Frequency-Dependent Noise Tolerance

Low-frequency visual information (1-10 Hz) preserved under high noise. High-frequency information degraded more rapidly. Aligns with biological visual system priorities.

High Information Efficiency

Retinal systems achieve ~70-80% of theoretical maximum information efficiency given biological noise. Comparable to engineered systems.

Receptive Field Optimization

Center-surround filtering provides 2-3× contrast enhancement. Organization principle is robust and fundamental to efficient visual encoding.

Emergent Functional Organization

Unsupervised machine learning identifies 3-4 distinct neural types matching known retinal cell classes. Functional organization emerges from firing statistics.

Research Focus

This project traces a central question through multiple analytical lenses: how does biological noise degrade the fidelity of visual information in the retina? Beginning with signal simulation and progressing through frequency-domain analysis, dimensionality reduction, machine learning, and information-theoretic modeling, this research builds a layered computational investigation of retinal information processing.
